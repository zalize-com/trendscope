# What HN actually asks about: 19 years of questions

We backfilled every month's top-10 Ask HN threads — 238 months, 2,378
questions, February 2007 through today (the `ask_hn` tag also sweeps in
Tell/Thank HN, which turns out to matter). The result says something about
what this community is for.

**The biggest questions are never technical.** Of the all-time top-40, 32
are human questions: careers ("longest-serving programmer, 57 years and
counting"), health ("I'm a software engineer going blind — how should I
prepare?"), the community talking to itself (S3 outage threads, dang
appreciation posts, policy pleas). A technical how-to barely cracks the
top-40. HN's most-upvoted asks are really a town square, not a forum.

**The ritual has three registers, and they grew at different times.**
Ask (1,572) dominates; Tell (228) is the confession/announcement channel
and didn't exist as a genre early on; Thank (23) is rare but peaks hard —
"You helped me get a new job" (2,425pts) outscores almost every technical
question ever asked.

**The #1 question of all time is a regulator asking permission.** An FCC
Commissioner posting "proposing regulation of IoT security — what do you
think?" (3,387pts) is the biggest Ask ever: the community's answer to
"does government listen?" was yes, once, in public.

**Questions age differently than stories.** Front-page canon = events
(deaths, dramas, outages). Show HN canon = artifacts. Ask canon = *people* —
the only leaderboard where the winning entry is usually a life, not a link.
